# Confidence, speed and quality

Use to choose the appropriate investment in learning versus refining a solution.
Supply evidence for problem importance and solution fit, plus the cost of being wrong.

## Procedure

1. Assess evidence for the problem separately from evidence for the proposed solution.
   Treat technical feasibility as another uncertainty where relevant, not proof of demand.
2. With weak problem evidence, favor a small learning exercise. With a well-supported
   problem but uncertain solution, test solution fit while protecting essential quality.
3. When both are supported, invest in durable quality proportionate to expected use.
   Confidence alone does not justify scale or extra features.
4. Name acceptable shortcuts, nonnegotiable constraints, a learning signal, and
   when to discard, revise, or invest further. Label thresholds proposed without data.

Output evidence by uncertainty, build posture, and a bounded next step. Stop once
the learning objective and investment boundary are clear. Do not treat subjective
percentages as measurements or low confidence as permission to skip safety,
privacy, or correctness needed by the experiment.

## Example and boundary

A feature has two enthusiastic comments but no usage evidence. Test a manual
workflow with consenting participants before building automation. Keep real data
protected and make limitations clear. If demand is established but the UI is untested,
test the UI; do not reopen demand research merely because solution confidence is low.
