# Eisenhower Matrix

Use to distinguish time pressure from importance. Supply tasks, desired outcomes,
deadlines, consequences of delay, and any real delegation options.

## Procedure

1. Define importance against the user's goals, obligations, and material consequences.
2. Assess urgency using deadlines or a concrete cost of waiting. Loudness is not urgency.
3. Place tasks into do now, schedule, consider delegating, or consider dropping.
4. Expose unknown deadlines and dependencies. A prerequisite may need attention
   before a more important task can proceed.
5. Recommend a short sequence appropriate to available capacity.

Output a table with importance, urgency, evidence, and proposed action. Stop once
the immediate queue and information gaps are clear. Delegation requires a real
capable recipient; without one, propose scheduling or reducing the task instead.

## Example and boundary

A grant closes tomorrow, strategic planning has no deadline, and an optional
survey closes today. If the grant supports the stated mission, do it now; schedule
planning; consider skipping the survey unless its value is established. Do not
invent a staff member to delegate it to or actually delete any task.
