# Chapter I: Start (Idea, Evaluation, Wedge, Naming)

Use when validating an idea, picking a first market, checking founder readiness,
or choosing a name and domain. Goal: solve the next week, not the next five
years.

## Readiness check

Starting is timely when the founder has extra time or risk tolerance plus at
least one signal:
- Seen a painful problem up close.
- Understands a customer or industry better than outsiders.
- Has access to a market others cannot easily reach.
- A new technology unlocks something previously too slow, costly, or awkward.
- Keeps returning to the same problem even after trying to ignore it.

## Idea sources and tests

1. Problems you understand:
   - Prior work (strongest source): brings customer language, failed tools, and
     cost breakdown.
   - Personal life: works best for frequent, obvious, shared pains.
   - Customer research: sharpens problems, but people describe symptoms and ask
     for features rather than root causes. Filter through your own judgment.
2. New technology: Map capability to existing pain. "We use AI" is not an idea;
   ask "what became newly possible that customers already wanted?"
3. Alpha (unfair advantage): Relationships, distribution channels, regulatory
   shifts, or insider workflow knowledge. Use alpha as a wedge, then prove
   repeatable value and retention.

## Four-lens evaluation

Evaluate every idea across four lenses using First Principles (separate
workaround facts from solution wishes) and Inversion ("how does this fail in
month 1?"):

| Lens               | Pass signal                                 | Red flag                            |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Market size        | Big proven spend OR small fast-growing      | Tiny ceiling with no expansion path |
| Founder/Market fit | Realistic shot to reach and serve buyer     | Domain gated without insider access |
| Pain               | Losing money/time/risk; paid workarounds    | "Nice to have" with zero budget     |
| Unique insight     | Clear "why now" most incumbents miss        | Identical copy with no edge         |

Workaround test: "What does the customer do today because your product does not
exist, and would they pay you to fix it?" Spreadsheets, extra hires, duct-taped
tools, consultants, and hated incumbents prove demand.

Avoid four common traps:
- Skipping hard ideas (hard problems repel clones and attract talent/capital).
- Skipping boring industries (logistics, insurance, construction, compliance,
  accounting, healthcare admin, manufacturing, legal have real budgets and old
  software).
- Fearing competitors (competition proves demand; ask "why choose us now?").
- Waiting for the perfect idea (1 month of customer talks and prototyping beats
  1 year of abstract brainstorming).

## Pick a wedge market

Apply Abstraction Laddering to step down from an un-sellable broad market to an
actionable first wedge:

- Too broad: "Small businesses" -> Reachable: "Independent dental practices" ->
  First wedge: "Independent dental practices with 3-10 locations fighting
  insurance claim denials."
- Too broad: "Creators" -> First wedge: "YouTube educators selling paid cohorts
  who need to convert viewers into students."

Six traits of a good first wedge:
1. Easy to describe in one sentence.
2. Frequent or expensive pain.
3. Buyer has real budget.
4. Reachable without a large sales team.
5. Current alternatives are bad, slow, expensive, or outdated.
6. Market change creates an opening now.

## Pick a name and domain

- Simple, memorable, spellable after hearing once, flexible beyond one tiny
  feature, distinct from incumbents, no explanation-needed cleverness.
- Check basic trademark conflict and social handles before getting attached.
- TLD rules: `.com` is ideal; `.co`, `.app`, `.io`, `.ai`, `.dev`, `.tv` (media),
  and `.xyz` (crypto) are acceptable. Avoid obscure TLDs (lower trust/ranking).
- Prefix trick (`try<name>`, `use<name>`) unblocks common domains fast. Never
  spend months negotiating a domain before talking to customers.

## Grill frontier and deliverable

If pain/workarounds, wedge specificity, or "why now" are missing, grill before
delivering. Output:
1. Verdict (Go / Narrow First / Reject) with evidence vs assumptions split.
2. Wedge Card (Buyer, Trigger/Pain, Current Workaround, Why Now).
3. Naming/Domain check (if requested).
4. One 7-day validation sprint (exact target count, interview ask, pass rule).
