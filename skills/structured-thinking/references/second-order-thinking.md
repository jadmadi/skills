# Second-order thinking

Use when an action changes incentives, behavior, or constraints beyond its initial
effect. Supply the proposal, affected parties, relevant timescale, and evidence.

## Procedure

1. Describe the immediate effect and conditions needed for it to occur.
2. Ask how people or systems could respond, and what that response changes next.
3. Distinguish plausible causal mechanisms from mere chronological sequences.
4. Consider beneficial and adverse branches, delays, reversibility, and constraints.
5. Recommend an action with signals to monitor and a condition for reconsideration.

Output a small consequence tree or table. Label downstream effects as hypotheses
unless observed. Stop when further branches become speculative or cease to change
the decision. Use context-appropriate horizons, not mandatory month counts.

## Example and boundary

Removing a review step may shorten queues. If defects then reach users, support
work could interrupt delivery and erase the initial gain. Neither result is certain.
Propose a bounded trial with review latency, escaped defects, and interruption time
measured. Do not predict exact savings without a baseline or launch the trial unasked.
