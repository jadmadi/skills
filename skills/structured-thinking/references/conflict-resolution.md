# Conflict Resolution Diagram

Use when two actions appear incompatible but may serve compatible needs. Supply
each party's stated demand, rationale, and constraints. Do not infer agreement.

## Procedure

1. Identify a shared objective A, or label a proposed objective for confirmation.
2. Name need B and need C, then action D serving B and action D' serving C.
3. Explain the claimed necessity of A←B, A←C, B←D, and C←D', and why D conflicts
   with D'. Surface the assumptions under every link, not just one side's position.
4. Challenge assumptions with evidence and generate an alternative that could
   satisfy both needs. Check whether the conflict is genuinely removed.
5. If no compatible option is supported, state the remaining tradeoff and who
   must decide. A compromise is not automatically a win-win result.

Output the A/B/C/D/D' map, assumptions, and a testable proposal. Stop at a viable
proposal or a clearly identified constraint. Do not contact parties or decide their values.

## Example and boundary

A team wants reliable releases. One need is fast feedback, another is controlled
exposure. Immediate full rollout conflicts with a long freeze. A staged rollout
might meet both if exposure can be bounded. If the system cannot isolate cohorts,
that proposal is not yet feasible; verify the capability instead of declaring victory.
