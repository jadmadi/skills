# Situation-Behavior-Impact

Use to draft feedback about a specific observed action. Supply when and where it
happened, the behavior, and its known effect. Ask for a missing material detail or
leave it explicitly unspecified; do not fabricate a believable incident.

## Procedure

1. State the specific situation so the recipient can identify the event.
2. Describe observable behavior, removing character labels and inferred intent.
3. Explain the reported impact. Qualitative effects are valid; numbers are not
   required. Distinguish an experienced effect from a suspected downstream cause.
4. Where useful, invite the recipient's perspective with an open question.
5. Match the requested tone and audience without weakening the factual boundary.

Output a ready-to-review draft, with factual gaps noted separately if necessary.
Stop when the message is specific, fair, and usable. Drafting does not authorize sending.

## Example and boundary

Input: "In Tuesday's demo Sam interrupted twice while I explained the results;
I lost my place." Draft: "During Tuesday's demo, you spoke over me twice while
I was explaining the results. I lost my place.
How did that part of the demo feel from your side?"
Do not convert this into "You don't respect me" or invent harm to the whole team.
