# Cynefin framework

Use to choose a response suited to how cause and effect can be understood. Supply
the situation, observed stability, relevant expertise, and limits on intervention.

## Procedure

1. Assess whether causality is established, analyzable, only discoverable through
   interaction, or currently obscured by instability. Classification is provisional.
2. Match the response: clear means apply established practice; complicated means
   investigate with relevant expertise; complex means bounded safe-to-fail probes;
   chaotic means stabilize first within authority; confused means separate parts
   and gather enough context to choose an approach.
3. Explain the evidence for the classification and alternatives. A missing log or
   unfamiliar technology does not by itself make a situation complex or chaotic.
4. Define the next action and observations that would require reassessment.

Output a provisional domain, evidence, and fitting response. Stop when this changes
the practical approach. Classify subproblems separately when the overall situation
spans domains. Do not turn the framework into a mandatory gate for every decision.

## Example and boundary

A repeatable calculation defect with available inputs is likely analyzable even
if its cause is currently unknown. Investigate it. Community adoption of a new
service may require small probes because behavior emerges through interaction.
An active outage may require stabilization before a full causal analysis.

## Source

Dave Snowden's framework, summarized by
[Untools](https://untools.co/cynefin-framework/). This guide uses clear and confused
where other versions use obvious/simple and disorder; apply the response logic,
not a claim that every version has identical terminology.
