# Six Thinking Hats

Use for a decision that benefits from separated perspectives. Supply the decision,
constraints, evidence, and any reported stakeholder reactions.

## Procedure

1. White: summarize known evidence and consequential gaps.
2. Red: record reported intuitions or reactions without turning them into facts.
   If none were supplied, say so; possible reactions are hypotheses to ask about.
3. Black: identify credible failure mechanisms and costs.
4. Yellow: identify benefits and the conditions needed to obtain them.
5. Green: explore alternatives, including a smaller change or keeping the status quo.
6. Blue: synthesize the decision, unresolved tradeoff, and next useful action.

Keep perspectives distinct without roleplaying people. Output a short labeled
comparison and synthesis. Stop when the decision or missing evidence is clear.

## Example and boundary

A library considers Sunday opening. A survey reports demand, but staffing costs
are unknown. White records both; yellow considers access; black considers staffing;
green suggests one trial Sunday. Red has no supplied feelings. Blue recommends
costing the trial before committing. Do not invent employee enthusiasm to fill a hat.

## Source

Edward de Bono's method, summarized by
[Untools](https://untools.co/six-thinking-hats/). The example is an original adaptation.
