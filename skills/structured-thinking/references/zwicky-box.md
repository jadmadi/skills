# Zwicky box

Use to explore configurations of a solution by varying its dimensions. Supply
the problem, hard constraints, and known design choices.

## Procedure

1. Choose dimensions that describe different decisions, such as delivery channel,
   timing, and staffing. Avoid dimensions that duplicate the same choice.
2. List plausible values for each dimension, including a useful unconventional
   value when appropriate. Distinguish existing capabilities from proposals.
3. Record dependencies and incompatible pairs. Independence is a useful modeling
   aim, not permission to ignore real constraints.
4. Assemble a bounded shortlist of combinations. Do not enumerate a huge Cartesian
   product or equate novelty with usefulness.
5. Compare configurations against the goal and select one or more useful tests.

Output the dimension table, compatibility rules, and shortlisted configurations.
Stop once the shortlist covers meaningfully different feasible approaches. Do not
claim feasibility merely because each individual component is feasible.

## Example and boundary

A community workshop varies format (in-person/remote), timing (live/self-paced),
and facilitation (volunteer/recorded). Recorded self-paced material is coherent;
"live with no available facilitator" violates a constraint. Shortlist feasible
combinations rather than presenting every combination as a viable innovation.
