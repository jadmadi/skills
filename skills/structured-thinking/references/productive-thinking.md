# Productive Thinking Model

Use to develop a creative response to a meaningful problem and turn it into an
action plan. Supply the problem, affected people, known evidence, and constraints.

## Procedure

1. What's going on? Establish the situation, effects, and desired future.
2. What's success? Define observable outcomes, restrictions, available investment,
   values, and essential results. Label proposed targets rather than inventing commitments.
3. What's the question? Frame questions whose answers could unlock that success.
4. Generate answers. Explore alternatives without prematurely rejecting them;
   retain useful variety rather than requiring a fixed idea count.
5. Forge the solution. Evaluate, combine, and improve candidates against step 2.
6. Align resources. Specify the next experiment, resources, proposed ownership,
   dependencies, and how to assess the result. Confirm unknown availability.

Output a concise six-stage account or a plan that makes all six outcomes visible.
Stop when a feasible next experiment and its success criteria are defined. Do not
skip problem framing or resource alignment, and do not execute the plan without authority.

## Example and boundary

A club wants more first-time visitors to return. Establish attendance evidence,
define a proposed return-rate goal, ask what prevents a second visit, generate
welcome options, select a small buddy trial, and identify volunteer capacity and
a measurement period. Do not assume the cause is poor welcome or assign named
volunteers without evidence that they have agreed.
