# Chapter III: Sell (Brand, Website, ICP, Sales Motion, Outbound, Marketing)

Use when refining brand/landing copy, running first-user tests, sharpening ICP,
setting up CRM/outbound cadences, preparing consult/pitch/close calls, fixing
funnel leaks, or choosing marketing channels.

## Brand and website v1

Brand needs three aligned pieces:
1. Story (why you exist: the concrete broken workflow you saw).
2. Operational values (beliefs that visibly shape product/policy, not posters).
3. Visual identity (consistent logo, palette, typography, layout, imagery).

Website v1 must answer five questions above the fold:
1. Who is this for?
2. What painful problem does it solve?
3. What does the product do?
4. Why is it better than the current way?
5. What should the visitor do next? (Live: Sign up, Book a demo, Log in; Early:
   Join waitlist, Talk to us).

## Get your first user (live screen-share test)

Do not drop a link and ask "what do you think?" Watch warm contacts use the
product live and test six questions (audit claims via Ladder of Inference):
1. Do they understand what it does within 60 seconds?
2. How many steps to the first valuable moment? (Every extra step risks drop.)
3. Where do they hesitate, misclick, or misread labels?
4. What pulls them back tomorrow or next week?
5. Would they pay for it? (Polite "maybe" = no.)
6. Who else has this exact problem? (Seeds first lead list.)

Users reveal problems well; do not blindly build their proposed UI solutions.

## ICP and competitor gaps

Transform broad descriptions into targetable profiles:
- Bad: "Ecommerce companies."
- Good: "DTC brands doing $5M-$50M revenue relying on repeat purchases with a
  small retention team."

Narrow across: company size, industry, growth stage, geography, workflow,
budget, and trigger event (funding, new exec, hiring spree, regulation shift,
broken process).

Competitor audit: Search main category keyword; examine homepage audience,
lead pain, highlighted features, pricing, and review complaints; identify what
incumbents miss (ignoring smaller teams, complex UX, outdated workflow) and
position in that gap.

## Sales motion selector (2x2)

Use a Decision Matrix across product complexity/price and buyer intent:

| Quadrant                  | Fit                                           |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Product-Led Inbound       | Simple, low risk, buyers already searching    |
| Sales-Assisted Inbound    | Complex, high trust, buyers already searching |
| Low-Touch Outbound        | Simple, clear ICP, buyers not looking yet     |
| Founder-Led Consultative  | Complex/risky/expensive, demand not created   |

Most startups begin in Outbound because nobody knows they exist yet.

## Outbound workflow, warmup, and 7-day cadence

1. Minimal CRM fields: Account, Decision Maker, Objective Stage, Last
   Discussion, Next Follow-Up, Win/Loss Reason.
2. Inbox warmup (mandatory on new domains): Real business domain, SPF + DKIM +
   DMARC configured, low bounce, clear opt-out, gradual send ramp (Day 1: 2
   emails, Day 2: 3, Day 3: 4, Day 4: 5). Never blast hundreds from day one.
3. Lead list rules: Relevant (matches ICP), Repeatable (rebuildable), Reachable
   (real contact), Rare (not a bought blast list).
   Score Tier 1 (strong ICP + clear trigger), Tier 2 (good ICP + some signal),
   Tier 3 (unclear need/long cycle). Spend time on Tier 1 and Tier 2.
4. Decision maker: Target problem fit + budget/influence + timing + familiarity,
   not blindly the highest title.
5. Non-spam cold email shape: Trigger observation -> named problem -> one-line
   how you solve it -> small next step. Cut "hope this email finds you well" and
   "revolutionary platform" fluff.

7-Day Outbound Cadence:
- Day 1: Email (context, trigger, problem, solve)
- Day 2: Social touch (become familiar)
- Day 3: Email (answer likely objection)
- Day 4: Social touch (meaningful engagement)
- Day 5: Email (share differentiator)
- Day 6: Connection / DM (relevant note)
- Day 7: Email (soft walkaway)

Repeat up to 3x across different decision makers at the account. Follow up at
1 month on fresh news; restart cadence at 2 months.

## Pipeline, consult, pitch, close, and leak diagnosis

8-Stage Pipeline (advance only on objective events; review weekly):
`Open -> Trying to Contact -> Contacted -> Consult -> Pitch -> Verbal Commit
-> Closed Won -> Closed Lost`.

7 Consult Call Questions:
1. What problem are you trying to solve?
2. Why does it matter now?
3. How do you handle it today?
4. What is broken about that approach?
5. What happens if you do nothing?
6. Who else is involved in the decision?
7. What does a successful outcome look like?

6-Part Pitch (Minto structure; sell outcomes, not features):
1. Here is what we heard (their words).
2. Here is why it matters (time, money, risk, growth).
3. Here is what changes (better workflow/outcome).
4. Here is how the product helps (minimum detail needed).
5. Here is proof (examples, data, tight demo).
6. Here is the next step.

Concrete Close (never "let me know what you think"): Lock price, scope, buyer,
approval process, timeline, onboarding plan, and next date (example: "Can we
send the order form today and schedule onboarding for next Tuesday?").

Monthly Funnel Leak Diagnosis (fix ONE variable per cycle):
- Leads -> Contacted weak: fix list quality, copy, or deliverability.
- Contacted -> Consult weak: message lacks ICP trigger relevance.
- Consult -> Pitch weak: targeting wrong ICP or non-urgent pain.
- Pitch -> Verbal Commit weak: story, proof, or offer is weak.
- Verbal Commit -> Closed Won weak: unclear pricing, trust, procurement, or
  onboarding.

## Pricing check (commercial goal mode)

Skip this section for `open_source` or `fun` goal modes. For commercial
products, set a concrete price before launch:
- Anchor price to the cost of the customer's current workaround (hours wasted,
  extra headcount, or lost revenue), not to server or token costs.
- Start with 1 to 2 simple tiers and charge from day one (paid pilot, deposit,
  or monthly plan). Treat "I would use it if it were free" as a `No`.
- Test the price live on consult and close calls; raise prices when close rates
  stay high and buyers rarely push back on cost.

## Marketing and consistent 5-part story

Match format to buyer (devs = technical guides/micro-apps; operators =
templates/checklists; execs = benchmarks; consumers = short video/proof). Taste
and one sharp idea beat high-volume fluff.

Pick ONE primary channel and ONE supporting channel from: SEO (compounding,
slow), Paid (fast signal, burns cash on weak funnels), Organic social/community
(trust, needs consistency), or Influencer/Partnerships (borrowed trust).
Measure content -> qualified visitors -> leads -> meetings -> customers.

Keep one 5-part Customer-Hero Story everywhere (site, outreach, calls, ads,
onboarding):
1. Customer has a goal.
2. Obstacle blocks them.
3. Old way fails.
4. Product creates a better path.
5. Customer gets a better outcome.
