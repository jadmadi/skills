# Reinforcing feedback loop

Use to examine a closed mechanism that amplifies an initial change. Supply the
variables, proposed causal mechanisms, observations, and boundaries.

## Procedure

1. Trace how a change in one variable affects others and returns to amplify that
   original change. Require a closed path; association or a one-way chain is insufficient.
2. Sign each link: positive for same-direction change, negative for opposite-direction
   change, other conditions held constant. An even number of negative links,
   including zero, makes the loop reinforcing.
3. Label evidence and assumptions per link. Reinforcing does not mean beneficial:
   the same structure can amplify growth or decline.
4. Identify delays, resource limits, competing balancing loops, and conditions
   under which the mechanism stops applying.
5. Suggest a bounded way to strengthen or interrupt the mechanism, and how to test it.

Output the signed loop, limits, and proposed check. Stop once the mechanism is
testable. Do not infer exponential growth, a doubling time, or the best intervention
from the loop's sign alone; those need quantitative evidence.

## Example and boundary

Useful contributions → project usefulness (+) → contributor interest (+) → useful
contributions (+) is a reinforcing hypothesis. Limited review capacity may cap it.
Do not predict unlimited growth. Two negative links can also reinforce: trust
reduces monitoring, and monitoring reduces trust, if both causal claims hold here.
