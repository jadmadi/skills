# Impact-Effort Matrix

Use to compare opportunities by their contribution to a goal and the work required.
Supply the goal, candidate actions, capacity, dependencies, and available estimates.

## Procedure

1. Define impact against a specific outcome and effort against a consistent unit
   or qualitative scale. Include coordination and recurring costs where relevant.
2. Estimate with evidence and uncertainty; use ranges or unknown where appropriate.
3. Place supported items into high-impact/low-effort, high/high, low/low, or low/high.
   Declare what high and low mean in this context. Leave uncertain placements tentative.
4. Account for prerequisites, mandatory work, risk, and available capacity before
   proposing an order. A dependency can outrank its apparent standalone impact.

Output a table of impact, effort, uncertainty, quadrant, and proposed order. Stop
when priorities or the estimates worth collecting are clear. Do not divide ordinal
ratings into a supposedly precise ROI or treat a low quadrant as permission to delete.

## Example and boundary

A broken signup link blocks known users and takes a small verified fix. A dashboard
redesign has unknown demand and several weeks of estimated work. Fix the link first;
validate dashboard demand before labeling it a major bet. A required accessibility
repair cannot be dropped solely because immediate revenue impact is unknown.
