# Concept map

Use to explain concepts and their relationships. Supply a domain, artifact, or
bounded collection of files and the audience's purpose.

## Procedure

1. Identify the central question and essential concepts. Use source terminology.
2. Connect concepts with explicit relationship phrases so each edge reads as a
   proposition, such as "member submits application."
3. Group related concepts and add useful cross-links. Do not force a hierarchy
   where the source describes peers or a network.
4. Mark ambiguous terms, inferred relationships, and missing evidence.
5. Highlight only hubs or gaps that matter to the question; many links alone do
   not prove importance, poor design, or causality.

Output a labeled edge list, with an optional Mermaid diagram. Stop when the map
serves the audience's question without becoming an exhaustive inventory.
Keep causal claims separate: "uses" and "belongs to" are not feedback mechanisms.

## Example and boundary

Given "a volunteer submits an application, a coordinator reviews it, and approved
volunteers join a team," create these three labeled propositions. Do not add a
background check or a database merely because similar systems often have one.
If the audience needs only onboarding, omit unrelated payroll concepts.
