# Hard choice model

Use to choose how much and what kind of decision work is useful. Supply the options,
stakes, and what makes them easy or difficult to compare.

## Procedure

1. Assess impact against actual consequences for this decision-maker.
2. Assess comparability: can the options be judged on meaningful shared criteria,
   or do they serve substantially different values?
3. Classify: low impact/easy comparison is a no-brainer; low impact/hard comparison
   is apples and oranges; high impact/easy comparison is a big choice; high impact/
   hard comparison is a hard choice. Explain uncertain or mixed classifications.
4. Match the process: choose quickly, clarify a preference, gather comparative
   evidence, or explore values and a bounded experiment, respectively.
5. Consider reversibility as a separate practical constraint, not a replacement
   axis. Let the user resolve value commitments that the evidence cannot settle.

Output the classification, its basis, and the appropriate next decision step.
Stop before building an unnecessary scoring exercise for incomparable values.

## Example and boundary

Choosing between a management role and research may be high-impact and difficult
to compare because the person values different kinds of work. Clarify priorities
or arrange a permitted trial. Do not label it a no-brainer merely because returning
to the previous role is possible, or invent which identity the person prefers.
